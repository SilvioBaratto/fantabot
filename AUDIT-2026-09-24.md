### 0.1 `rules_for_lega` drops `max_roles` on the Mantra path **[re-verified]**
`src/fantabot/domain/asta/state.py:137-146`

`max_roles` is read **only inside `if classic:`**. The Mantra return builds
`RosterRules(size, min_goalkeepers, min_movement)` — and `RosterRules` has no field for it
at all (`['size','goalkeeper_roles','min_goalkeepers','min_movement']`).
`max_goalkeepers()` is derived as `size - min_movement`.

Measured against lega 4103937's live snapshot:

    rules_for_lega(role_groups=2, roster_size=32, min_roles=[2,23], max_roles=[4,28])
      max_goalkeepers() -> 9     lega declares maxrl[0] = 4
      max_movement()    -> 30    lega declares maxrl[1] = 28

Consumers, both fail-open: `optimizer.py:360` (hard ceiling on drafting a keeper) and
`reservation.py:150` (the walk-away). Nothing catches it — not `max_bid`, not `lot_ceiling`,
and per `docs/fantalab/01:142` not the server: the MAX cap is client-enforced.

This is the 2026-09-02 roster-rules drift CLAUDE.md:261-271 recorded. `rules_for_lega` was
taught to read `minrl` and never `maxrl`.

Invisible to the suite: `tests/domain/asta/test_asta_rules_for_lega.py:50-56` passes
`max_roles=[4, 28]` and asserts nothing about it.

### 0.2 `because(exc)` 500s on any empty-message exception **[re-verified]**
`app/fantabot_app/api/outcomes.py:141`, mirrored at `api/v1/endpoints/room.py:79-82`

    return f"{type(exc).__name__}: {str(exc).splitlines()[0]}"

`str(OSError())`, `str(TimeoutError())` and a bare `SQLAlchemyError()` are all `""`, and

Called from `asta.py:294,654`, `lineup.py:156,289,567`, `teams.py:168,190`,
`exclusions.py:133`, `room.py:120,131`. The only test passes a 3-line message.

Fix: `(str(exc).splitlines() or [""])[0]`.
